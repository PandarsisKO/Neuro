# Golden: Buying a Small Business — Masterplan

## Purpose
To provide a comprehensive framework for acquiring a small main-street business (HVAC, services, light manufacturing) in the US using SBA 7(a) financing, covering financing mechanics and down payment requirements, business valuation methods, due diligence scope, deal structure from letter of intent through closing, and the critical first 90 days of ownership.

## Executive Summary

Buying a small business with an SBA 7(a) loan requires 10% down in cash or seller debt (capped at 5% real cash if structured on full standby), with the lender guaranteeing 75–85% of the loan amount depending on size. Valuation relies on seller's discretionary earnings (SDE) multiples of 2.5–4x for businesses under $1M in earnings; multiples are pushed higher by assignable contracts and professional management, and lower by owner dependence and poor record-keeping. Due diligence must verify SDE through a qualified opinion of earnings (QoE), check all contracts and licenses for assignability, confirm no single customer exceeds 20% of revenue, and establish a working capital baseline. Deal structure includes an LOI with explicit SDE figure and exclusivity window, a purchase agreement with 90-day seller transition and holdback provisions, and immediate transfer of banking, payroll, and insurance on closing day. The first 90 days focus on validating business continuity with staff, implementing quick employee-suggested improvements, and ensuring the seller's committed hours of transition support are being fulfilled.

## Key Insights

### Financing Structure and Down Payment Rules

**Loan Guarantee and Size Limits**  
[SBA guarantees 75% of loans above $150,000 and 85% of loans at or below that amount.](file://report_sba_summary.pdf) Deals larger than SBA caps require conventional financing or a blended structure. [Deals larger than that require conventional financing or a blended structure.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00001&t=75s)

**Minimum Equity Injection**  
[This is the minimum down payment requirement for a complete change-of-ownership acquisition loan.](file://report_sba_summary.pdf) [On a $1M purchase you must bring $100,000 cash/equity to closing.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00001&t=6s) However, [up to half of the 10% equity injection can be structured as a seller note instead of buyer cash, cutting real cash needed to 5%.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00001&t=27s)

**Seller Debt and Standby Requirements**  
[Seller debt only counts toward the required equity injection if fully on standby (no principal or interest payments) for the entire 7(a) loan term, capped at half the injection.](file://report_sba_summary.pdf) [The seller receives zero payments—no principal, no interest—until the SBA loan is fully repaid, for the entire loan term.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00001&t=39s) Critically, [lenders won't count the seller note toward the buyer's required equity unless it is put on full standby, which most sellers refuse.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00003&t=2743s)

**Interest Rates and Fees**  
[Rate runs at roughly prime plus 2.5–3%.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00001&t=47s) [This fee is charged on the guaranteed portion of the loan, varies by loan size and maturity, and can be rolled into the financed loan amount.](file://report_sba_summary.pdf) [This fee can be financed into the loan rather than paid out of pocket.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00001&t=118s)

**Rate and Timeline Variability**  
[SBA lender rates can differ by a full percentage point and closing timelines by a month, so shopping around matters.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00003&t=2991s)

**Personal Guarantee**  
[Anyone owning a fifth or more of the business is personally on the hook with no cap, a key risk to weigh before signing the LOI.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00001&t=60s)

**Real Estate Component**  
[If the target business owns its building, financing the real estate separately on a longer amortization lowers the monthly payment significantly.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00003&t=3494s) [If real estate is the largest component of the deal, maturity can extend up to 25 years instead.](file://report_sba_summary.pdf) [Timeline extends further if the target business owns real estate.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00001&t=104s)

**Collateral Requirements**  
[The lender must still take whatever collateral is available, including the acquired business's assets.](file://report_sba_summary.pdf) [Collateral shortfalls alone can't kill the loan, but the business's assets must be pledged as available collateral first.](file://report_sba_summary.pdf)

### Business Valuation

**SDE vs. EBITDA**  
[SDE equals net profit plus owner salary, benefits, and personal/one-time expenses run through the business, used for smaller main-street deals.](https://example.com/blog/how-small-businesses-are-valued) [Once a business has professional management and over roughly $1M in earnings, buyers must factor in paying a manager, so valuation shifts to EBITDA.](https://example.com/blog/how-small-businesses-are-valued)

**Valuation Multiples for SDE Businesses**  
[This is the typical range across the main-street market for businesses valued on SDE.](https://example.com/blog/how-small-businesses-are-valued) [Businesses under $1M in seller's discretionary earnings typically sell in this range, and anything above 4x needs a special justification.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00003&t=1995s)

**Factors Moving Multiples**  
[Assignable contracts, an in-place general manager, and reviewed financials command the top of the multiple range.](https://example.com/blog/how-small-businesses-are-valued) Conversely, [owner-dependent relationships, cash sales, and shoebox receipts push a business to the bottom of or below the multiple range.](https://example.com/blog/how-small-businesses-are-valued) [Revenue transferability, owner dependence, and books cleanliness push the multiple up or down within the range.](https://example.com/blog/how-small-businesses-are-valued)

**Higher Multiples in Specific Sectors**  
[He says private equity roll-ups are bidding up HVAC/plumbing valuations well above the commonly cited 2-3x earnings benchmark.](https://example.com/podcasts/no-money-down.mp3#t=49) [Given the elevated multiples PE is paying, he advises sellers in these trades against settling for the traditional lower multiple.](https://example.com/podcasts/no-money-down.mp3#t=60)

**Higher-Sized Business Multiples**  
[Businesses with more than $1 million of EBITDA typically trade in this higher range.](https://example.com/blog/how-small-businesses-are-valued)

**Working Capital Baseline**  
[This peg (normal receivables plus inventory minus payables) stops a seller from stripping out cash by collecting all receivables right before closing.](https://example.com/blog/how-small-businesses-are-valued) [First-time buyers often forget this and end up buying a business with an empty bank account; a normal peg is trailing twelve-month average AR plus inventory minus AP.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00002&t=81s)

**Accounts Receivable in Deal Terms**  
[AR transfer is not standard and must be negotiated separately deal by deal.](https://example.com/blog/how-small-businesses-are-valued)

**Explicit SDE in LOI**  
[Explicitly stating the SDE figure the offer is based on prevents later disputes over valuation.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00003&t=1753s)

### Due Diligence Requirements

**Qualified Opinion of Earnings (QoE)**  
[It verifies the seller's discretionary earnings by tying tax returns to bank statements and typically costs $8k-$20k for businesses under $5M revenue.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00002&t=17s)

**SDE Add-Back Scrutiny**  
[Sellers often add back salary, truck, and cell phone to inflate earnings, but only expenses that genuinely vanish when the seller leaves should count.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00002&t=40s)

**Alternative to Full QoE**  
[For smaller deals he substitutes a bookkeeper's review of bank statements for a full QoE report, saving about $15,000, diverging from standard due-diligence practice.](https://example.com/podcasts/no-money-down.mp3#t=68)

**Contractual Assignability**  
[Every lease, contract, and license must be checked for assignability, as this issue has ended more deals than bad financials.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00002&t=111s)

**Customer Concentration**  
[If one customer exceeds 20% of revenue, that risk must be priced into the deal or the buyer should walk; request three years of customer revenue data and check churn beyond the top ten.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00002&t=64s) [This is the one piece of conventional diligence advice he agrees with, treating customer concentration above 20% as a hard dealbreaker.](https://example.com/podcasts/no-money-down.mp3#t=80)

**Pre-Close Diligence with Seller Permission**  
[Do this with the seller's permission before closing to validate the business independently of seller-provided numbers.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00002&t=105s)

**DSCR and Cash Flow Verification**  
[This DSCR minimum is typically measured on a trailing twelve-month historical and projected cash flow basis and is a core underwriting/diligence checkpoint.](file://report_sba_summary.pdf) [Cash flow must cover the loan payment with 25% cushion for the deal to underwrite.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00001&t=86s) [An SBA lender independently checks that business cash flow covers loan payments at this minimum ratio, and will reject a price as too high for that financing if it fails, regardless of comparable sales.](https://example.com/blog/how-small-businesses-are-valued)

**Lender Industry Experience Requirement**  
[Lenders typically won't approve a loan unless the buyer or an existing manager has relevant industry background.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00001&t=98s)

## Recommended Actions / How to Use These Insights

1. **Establish your buyer profile and search strategy**: Document who you are, what you're looking for (business type, location, size, industry), and confirm you have (or can hire) someone with relevant industry experience, as lenders require this. Prioritize cold outreach to owners over BizBuySell-type listings.

2. **Build your equity and understand your real cash requirement**: Calculate 10% of the target purchase price. Understand that up to 5% can come from a seller note on full standby (no payments until SBA loan is repaid), meaning your real cash requirement may be as low as 5% for a structured deal.

3. **Confirm SBA loan terms upfront**: Shop rates across at least 2–3 lenders; differences can be 1% in rate and a full month in closing timeline. Ensure you understand the personal guarantee risk for all owners holding 20%+ of the business.

4. **Structure the seller profile and valuation anchor**: Before engaging in serious negotiations, establish the business's SDE using recent tax returns and P&L. Place the business into a multiple range (2.5–4x for sub-$1M SDE businesses) based on customer concentration, contract assignability, owner dependence, and book quality. Use this to set your opening offer price and ensure the SDE figure is explicit in the LOI.

5. **Commission a QoE or equivalent validation**: Even if using a bookkeeper's bank statement review instead of full QoE, validate that the seller's claimed earnings match actual tax filings and bank deposits. Budget $8k–$20k for a full QoE if the deal warrants it.

6. **Demand contractual and customer diligence**: Pull three years of customer revenue data and identify any customer exceeding 20% of revenue as a dealbreaker or a price-reduction trigger. Have counsel check every lease, service contract, license, and customer agreement for assignability; treat assignability failures as deal-ending issues.

7. **Establish working capital baseline**: Calculate trailing twelve-month average (AR + inventory − AP) and write this peg into the LOI and purchase agreement to prevent the seller from stripping cash right before closing.

8. **Structure the LOI with realistic exclusivity and working capital language**: Set an exclusivity window long enough to complete diligence (typically 30–45 days) but not so long that the seller loses confidence. Write in the working capital concept even if the exact figure is calculated later.

9. **Negotiate 90-day seller transition with holdback**: Make the seller's 90-day post-close availability and committed hours per week non-negotiable. Tie a holdback (typically 5–10% of purchase price) to the seller's satisfactory completion of transition duties.

10. **Prepare for closing day logistics**: Coordinate with the lender, attorney, and seller in advance to ensure bank signers, merchant account, payroll administration, and insurance are all transferred on the day of closing, not delayed. This prevents operational disruption.

11. **Implement quick wins in the first 90 days**: On your first day, gather employee input on what they'd fix. Immediately implement the cheapest suggestions to build credibility and signal change. Do not make staff changes except for safety issues during the first 90 days.

12. **Monitor the seller's transition commitment**: Track the seller's hours and responsiveness during the 90-day period. Use this to inform any holdback payment decision.

## Open Questions and Gaps

1. **Tax and entity structure implications**: The materials do not address whether to acquire assets or stock, how to structure the deal entity (LLC, S-corp, C-corp), or the tax implications of each choice.

2. **SBA loan contingencies and backup financing**: If SBA underwriting falls through or timelines slip, what is the backup financing plan? Is there a hard stop date where the buyer walks or pivots to conventional financing?

3. **Environmental and regulatory compliance**: For HVAC, services, and light manufacturing, what environmental, licensing, and regulatory due diligence must be conducted? The materials focus on financials and contracts but not compliance.

4. **Insurance and liability transition**: How should insurance be handled during the transition? What indemnification or tail coverage should the buyer require from the seller?

5. **Working capital adjustment mechanics post-close**: If working capital drifts from the peg after closing, what is the adjustment mechanism? Should this be included in the purchase agreement?

6. **Earn-out or seller contingency scenarios**: Should the seller note be contingent on business performance or customer retention? Are earn-outs ever used in these smaller deals?

7. **Detailed first 90 days playbook**: Beyond employee input and quick wins, what operational changes (pricing, service offerings, technology, staffing restructure) should a new owner prioritize?

8. **DSCR calculation methodology**: The materials state that cash flow must cover loan payments at 1.25x DSCR but do not detail whether this uses historical or projected cash flow, seasonal adjustments, or how the lender treats one-time or cyclical items.

9. **Customer retention and transition risk mitigation**: Beyond the seller's on-site hours, what contractual or operational measures can a buyer implement to lock in customers during transition?

10. **Comparison of seller financing vs. SBA approaches**: The podcast presents full seller financing as an alternative to SBA loans, but does not compare total cost, timeline, flexibility, or risk across both structures for specific deal profiles.

## How to Continue

1. **Add a tax and entity structuring guide**: Interview a CPA or M&A tax specialist to document asset vs. stock acquisition trade-offs, entity selection rationale, and post-close tax planning for small business acquisitions.

2. **Build a 90-day operations playbook**: Interview successful small business acquirers and operators in HVAC, services, and light manufacturing to create a detailed framework for the first 90 days—including customer retention, pricing review, service delivery standardization, and team reshaping.

3. **Create a due diligence checklist template**: Convert the diligence requirements into a working checklist with responsible parties, timelines, and red flags for each category (financials, contracts, customers, licenses, environmental, insurance).

4. **Research financing alternatives for edge cases**: Document when full seller financing, conventional bank loans, or SBA Loans Blended with other instruments are preferable to straight 7(a), with cost and risk comparisons.

5. **Add regulatory and environmental due diligence**: For HVAC and services, research EPA, state licensing, worker classification, and liability compliance requirements that must be audited during diligence.

6. **Interview post-close operators**: Capture 2–3 case studies of acquisitions that closed in the last 12 months, documenting what went well, what was unexpected, and how the first 90 days actually unfolded vs. plan.

7. **Build a working capital peg calculator**: Create a spreadsheet template that automatically pulls trailing twelve-month AR, inventory, and AP data from financial statements and calculates the working capital baseline and adjustment scenarios.

8. **Explore SBA loan vs. full seller financing comparison**: Commission or source a side-by-side financial analysis (including interest cost, personal guarantee risk, timeline, flexibility) of $500k, $1M, and $2M deal sizes under both financing structures.
