# Golden: Buying a Small Business — Masterplan

## Purpose

This project exists to answer a practical question for a prospective acquirer: how does one actually buy a small main-street business — an HVAC company, a services firm, a light manufacturer — in the United States using an SBA 7(a) loan? The brief calls for a working understanding of five things: the financing structure and down payment rules, how these businesses get valued, what due diligence must cover, how the deal itself should be structured (LOI, working capital, seller transition), and what to prioritize in the first 90 days of ownership.

## Executive summary

Buying a small business via SBA 7(a) financing follows a fairly standardized playbook. The buyer typically needs to bring 10% of total project cost as an equity injection, though seller carry-back notes (if placed on full standby) can satisfy up to half of that requirement, cutting real cash-to-close meaningfully [SBA 7(a) acquisition lending summary @ p. 2](file://report_sba_summary.pdf), [SBA 7(a) loans for buying a business, explained @ 0:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00001&t=27s). Loans run at prime plus 2.5–3%, are capped at 10 years without real estate (25 years with), and every 20%+ owner must personally guarantee the debt without limit [SBA 7(a) loans for buying a business, explained @ 0:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00001&t=47s), [SBA 7(a) acquisition lending summary @ p. 2](file://report_sba_summary.pdf), [SBA 7(a) acquisition lending summary @ p. 3](file://report_sba_summary.pdf). Valuation for main-street deals centers on Seller's Discretionary Earnings (SDE) multiples, shifting to EBITDA multiples once a business clears roughly $1M in earnings and requires professional management [How small businesses are valued: SDE and EBITDA multiples explained @ § 1](https://example.com/blog/how-small-businesses-are-valued). Regardless of what multiple the buyer and seller agree on, the lender independently underwrites to a minimum 1.25x debt service coverage ratio — and if cash flow doesn't clear that bar, the deal isn't financeable at that price [How small businesses are valued: SDE and EBITDA multiples explained @ § 2](https://example.com/blog/how-small-businesses-are-valued), [SBA 7(a) acquisition lending summary @ p. 3](file://report_sba_summary.pdf). Due diligence must go beyond the financials — verifying earnings via a Quality of Earnings (QoE) review, checking customer concentration, and confirming lease assignability, which alone has killed more deals than bad numbers [Due diligence checklist for a small business purchase @ 1:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00002&t=111s). Deal structure hinges on a working capital peg to prevent the seller from stripping cash before close, and a well-drafted LOI that locks in exclusivity and confidentiality even though most other terms remain negotiable [Full deal walkthrough: search to first 90 days (92 min) @ 21:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00003&t=1260s), [Full deal walkthrough: search to first 90 days (92 min) @ 37:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00003&t=2255s). Finally, the first 90 days should be about stability, not change — retaining the seller for a transition period, avoiding staff changes except for safety issues, and prioritizing revenue retention over optimization [Full deal walkthrough: search to first 90 days (92 min) @ 1:27:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00003&t=5271s). One source in the findings pushes back hard on this entire framework, arguing SBA financing itself is a trap not worth the personal guarantee and paperwork burden — a useful counterweight to keep in mind even if the mainstream playbook is followed [No money down: a contrarian on buying businesses @ 0:33](https://example.com/podcasts/no-money-down.mp3#t=33).

## Key insights

### Financing structure and down payment rules

- The core down payment rule for a full change of ownership: the borrower's minimum required equity injection is **10% of total project cost** [SBA 7(a) acquisition lending summary @ p. 2](file://report_sba_summary.pdf). On a $1M purchase, this translates to $100,000 cash the buyer must bring to closing at minimum [SBA 7(a) loans for buying a business, explained @ 0:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00001&t=6s).
- Seller carry-back debt can count toward that equity injection, but only if it is placed on **full standby** (no principal or interest payments) for the entire loan term, and only up to **50% of the required injection** [SBA 7(a) acquisition lending summary @ p. 2](file://report_sba_summary.pdf). In practice this means up to 5% of the purchase price can be financed via a seller note instead of cash, cutting real cash-to-close roughly in half [SBA 7(a) loans for buying a business, explained @ 0:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00001&t=27s). While on standby, the seller receives zero payments — no principal, no interest — until the SBA loan is fully paid off [SBA 7(a) loans for buying a business, explained @ 0:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00001&t=39s).
- The SBA guarantees 75% of loans above $150,000 and 85% of loans at or below that threshold [SBA 7(a) acquisition lending summary @ p. 1](file://report_sba_summary.pdf). The upfront guarantee fee scales with loan size and maturity but can be rolled into the financed loan amount rather than paid in cash [SBA 7(a) acquisition lending summary @ p. 3](file://report_sba_summary.pdf), [SBA 7(a) loans for buying a business, explained @ 1:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00001&t=118s).
- Rates typically run **prime plus 2.5–3%** [SBA 7(a) loans for buying a business, explained @ 0:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00001&t=47s). Maturity is capped at 10 years for loans without real estate, extending up to 25 years if real estate is the largest component of the deal [SBA 7(a) acquisition lending summary @ p. 2](file://report_sba_summary.pdf).
- Every owner holding 20% or more of the business must personally guarantee the loan without limit — a deal isn't automatically killed for lacking collateral, but this guarantee is unavoidable and is a key risk to weigh before signing an LOI [SBA 7(a) acquisition lending summary @ p. 3](file://report_sba_summary.pdf), [SBA 7(a) loans for buying a business, explained @ 1:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00001&t=60s).
- Underwriting requires that trailing-twelve-month cash flow (historical and projected) cover proposed debt payments by at least **1.25x**, i.e., a 25% cushion — this is non-negotiable regardless of the agreed sale price [SBA 7(a) acquisition lending summary @ p. 3](file://report_sba_summary.pdf), [SBA 7(a) loans for buying a business, explained @ 1:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00001&t=86s).
- Deals larger than $5M require conventional financing or a blended structure instead of pure SBA 7(a) [SBA 7(a) loans for buying a business, explained @ 1:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00001&t=75s).
- Most lenders want the buyer to have relevant industry experience or an existing management team ready to run the business post-close [SBA 7(a) acquisition lending summary @ p. 3](file://report_sba_summary.pdf, cross-referenced with [SBA 7(a) loans for buying a business, explained @ 1:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00001&t=98s)).
- Practical tip: compare lenders before committing, since rates can differ by a full percentage point and closing timelines by a month [Full deal walkthrough: search to first 90 days (92 min) @ 49:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00003&t=2991s). Buyers should prepare the core SBA loan application documents in advance [Full deal walkthrough: search to first 90 days (92 min) @ 54:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00003&t=3256s).

### Valuation: SDE and EBITDA multiples

- **SDE (Seller's Discretionary Earnings)** equals net profit plus owner salary, benefits, and one-time/personal expenses run through the business — this is the standard valuation metric for smaller main-street deals [How small businesses are valued: SDE and EBITDA multiples explained @ § 1](https://example.com/blog/how-small-businesses-are-valued).
- Once earnings exceed roughly $1M and the business requires professional management, buyers must budget for a manager's salary, so pricing conventionally shifts from SDE multiples to **EBITDA multiples**, which command a higher multiple range [How small businesses are valued: SDE and EBITDA multiples explained @ § 1](https://example.com/blog/how-small-businesses-are-valued).
- Where a business lands within its multiple range depends on revenue transferability, owner dependence, and book-cleanliness: contracts, a GM already in place, and reviewed financials push valuation to the top of the range; owner-dependent customer relationships and cash/shoebox bookkeeping push it to or below the bottom [How small businesses are valued: SDE and EBITDA multiples explained @ § 1](https://example.com/blog/how-small-businesses-are-valued).
- Multiples above 4x require special justification such as strong recurring revenue or a demonstrated growth trajectory [Full deal walkthrough: search to first 90 days (92 min) @ 33:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00003&t=1995s).
- The quoted deal price convention excludes cash but assumes operating inventory is included; receivables transfer is negotiated deal by deal [How small businesses are valued: SDE and EBITDA multiples explained @ § 2](https://example.com/blog/how-small-businesses-are-valued).
- Critically, **the lender's underwriting is the real ceiling on price**: regardless of comparable sales data or the multiple the parties agree to, the lender independently checks whether cash flow covers proposed loan payments at a minimum 1.25 DSCR — if it doesn't, the price is simply too high for SBA financing [How small businesses are valued: SDE and EBITDA multiples explained @ § 2](https://example.com/blog/how-small-businesses-are-valued).
- A contrarian claim worth flagging: private equity roll-ups are said to be pushing HVAC/plumbing valuations well above the traditional 2–3x earnings benchmark, which could compress buyer returns in that specific sector [No money down: a contrarian on buying businesses @ 0:49](https://example.com/podcasts/no-money-down.mp3#t=49).

### Due diligence

- A **Quality of Earnings (QoE) report** verifies the seller's discretionary earnings by tying tax returns to bank statements, costing $8,000–$20,000 for businesses under $5M revenue [Due diligence checklist for a small business purchase @ 0:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00002&t=8s).
- Sellers commonly inflate earnings via add-backs (salary, truck, cell phone expenses); a legitimate add-back is one that genuinely disappears once the seller leaves — nothing else should be counted [Due diligence checklist for a small business purchase @ 0:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00002&t=40s).
- **Customer concentration** above 20% from a single customer is treated as a major red flag: that risk must be explicitly priced into the deal, or the buyer should walk away [Due diligence checklist for a small business purchase @ 1:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00002&t=64s). Notably, even the contrarian source agrees this is an automatic deal-killer, showing rare consensus across otherwise opposing viewpoints [No money down: a contrarian on buying businesses @ 1:20](https://example.com/podcasts/no-money-down.mp3#t=80).
- The **working capital peg** is essential and often overlooked by first-time buyers, who risk acquiring a business with no operating cash if they don't insist on it [Due diligence checklist for a small business purchase @ 1:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00002&t=81s).
- **Lease assignability** is one of the single biggest deal risks: non-assignable leases have killed more deals than bad financials, making this a top-priority diligence check [Due diligence checklist for a small business purchase @ 1:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00002&t=111s).
- Diligence should also include direct validation of business health beyond the financials, conducted with the seller's permission before closing [Due diligence checklist for a small business purchase @ 1:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00002&t=105s).
- Contrarian counterpoint: for deals under $2M, a bookkeeper's review of bank statements can substitute for a full QoE report, saving roughly $15,000 — a meaningful cost-cutting option worth weighing against the risk of skipping formal QoE [No money down: a contrarian on buying businesses @ 1:08](https://example.com/podcasts/no-money-down.mp3#t=68).

### Deal structure: LOI, working capital, seller transition

- Sourcing tip: broker listings on BizBuySell exist, but the best deals reportedly came from directly contacting owners over 55 — suggesting off-market outreach may outperform browsing listings [Full deal walkthrough: search to first 90 days (92 min) @ 12:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00003&t=738s).
- In the LOI, almost every term can be renegotiated later, **except** exclusivity and confidentiality clauses, which are binding [Full deal walkthrough: search to first 90 days (92 min) @ 21:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00003&t=1260s).
- A 60-day exclusivity window is considered sufficient time to complete diligence while preventing the seller from shopping the offer elsewhere [Full deal walkthrough: search to first 90 days (92 min) @ 25:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00003&t=1500s).
- The LOI should explicitly state the SDE figure the offer is based on, to prevent disputes later in the process [Full deal walkthrough: search to first 90 days (92 min) @ 29:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00003&t=1753s).
- The **working capital peg** (normal level of receivables plus inventory minus payables that the seller must deliver at close) should be referenced in the LOI even before the exact number is determined, to avoid renegotiation friction later [Full deal walkthrough: search to first 90 days (92 min) @ 37:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00003&t=2255s). Its purpose is to prevent a seller from stripping cash by collecting all receivables right before closing [How small businesses are valued: SDE and EBITDA multiples explained @ § 2](https://example.com/blog/how-small-businesses-are-valued).
- Note a structural constraint: a seller note is only usable as part of the buyer's SBA equity injection if placed on full standby — and most sellers refuse this condition, which can complicate the "preferred capital stack" in practice [Full deal walkthrough: search to first 90 days (92 min) @ 45:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00003&t=2743s).
- Standard practice is to keep the seller involved for **90 days post-close**, with this transition period secured contractually via a holdback in the purchase agreement [Due diligence checklist for a small business purchase @ 2:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00002&t=124s).
- Contrarian alternative structure: rather than a down payment at all, some propose offering the seller full asking price paid out over five to seven years from the business's own future cash flow — effectively an extended, unsecured full seller-finance structure, distinct from the SBA-blended approach [No money down: a contrarian on buying businesses @ 0:09](https://example.com/podcasts/no-money-down.mp3#t=9).

### The first 90 days

- The core priority immediately post-close is **retaining existing revenue**, not growth or optimization — stabilization comes first [Full deal walkthrough: search to first 90 days (92 min) @ 1:27:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00003&t=5271s).
- Preserve the status quo with staff: the only exception to a no-firing rule during transition is an active safety issue [Full deal walkthrough: search to first 90 days (92 min) @ 1:23:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=golden00003&t=5021s).
